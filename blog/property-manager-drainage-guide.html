The Commercial Property Manager's Complete Guide to Drainage Decisions

January 23, 20268 min read

Property managers are expected to make intelligent decisions about drainage infrastructure without always having an engineering background. This guide cuts through the technical complexity and gives you a practical framework for evaluating drainage problems, understanding your options, and managing the decision process — whether you're dealing with a single-tenant industrial building or a large mixed-use portfolio.

How to Diagnose a Drainage Problem

Before calling anyone, document what you're seeing. Good documentation saves time, money, and arguments with contractors. Look for:

  • Where water stands and for how long after a rain event
  • Whether the problem is getting worse over time (suggests progressive clogging or settling)
  • Whether the problem is localized or widespread across the property
  • Whether existing drains are visibly clogged, collapsed, or absent
  • Whether water is entering any structures
  • The age of existing drainage infrastructure

Photos and video taken during or immediately after rain events are invaluable. Many drainage contractors won't properly diagnose a problem they haven't observed directly — your documentation fills that gap.

Understanding Your Options

Commercial drainage solutions range from temporary band-aids to permanent infrastructure. Understanding the difference helps you have better conversations with contractors:

  • Surface regrading: Adjusting site grades to redirect surface flow. Works only when the problem is truly a grading issue, not a soil absorption issue.
  • Catch basin cleaning and repair: Appropriate when existing infrastructure is functional but maintained. Not a solution when the underlying system design is inadequate.
  • French drain installation: Traditional solution with well-known failure modes. Appropriate for lower-volume applications with adequate slope. Plan for 5–10 year replacement cycles.
  • Hydro Fix pressure-fed systems: Permanent solution for both sloped and flat sites. Higher upfront clarity, zero maintenance cost, 25+ year life expectancy. Best for chronic problems and sites where repeated repairs are creating ongoing cost.
  • Stormwater retention systems: Required in many jurisdictions for compliance. Hydro Fix stormwater boxes provide below-grade retention that meets regulatory requirements.

How to Evaluate a Drainage Contractor

Not all drainage contractors are equal. Before engaging anyone, ask:

  • Do they start with a site evaluation before proposing a solution? (Red flag if they quote without seeing the problem in person)
  • Can they explain why the current system failed? (If they can't diagnose the cause, they can't fix it permanently)
  • What is the expected life of their proposed solution?
  • What maintenance will be required after installation?
  • Do they carry proper licensing and insurance for commercial work in Georgia?

Managing the Installation

Drainage installation in operating commercial properties requires coordination. Key considerations:

  • Notify tenants in advance of work that affects parking or access
  • Sequence work to minimize disruption — most professional drainage contractors can phase work around operational needs
  • Document the as-built system with photos and drawings for your property records
  • Confirm warranty terms in writing before installation begins

Building a Long-Term Drainage Maintenance Plan

Even permanent systems benefit from annual inspection. A simple annual walkthrough during or after a heavy rain event, looking for new wet spots or changes in drainage patterns, is sufficient for most Hydro Fix installations. Document observations and keep them in your property file for lender, insurance, and tenant reference.

The best drainage decision is one you make once and don't have to revisit for 25 years. That starts with understanding the problem before selecting the solution.
