Why this works when nothing else has

The Problem With Every System You've Ever Tried.

Every drainage system you've seen before relies on gravity to move water. That means slope, open gravel voids, and pipes that don't crush. In Georgia's clay soil, none of those assumptions hold long-term.

Hydro Fix uses a pressure-fed drainage system — the same technology used in large-scale municipal and commercial infrastructure. Water moves plank-to-plank via hydrostatic pressure, not gravity. That means it works on flat yards, clay soil, and every site where traditional systems fail. The internal structure never clogs, never crushes, and never needs to be serviced.

Is the evaluation really free with no obligation?
100%. We don't charge for evaluations and there's no obligation to proceed. If your property isn't a fit, we'll tell you and suggest alternatives. We make our money on installations that actually solve the problem — not on sales calls.
How long does the evaluation take?
Most evaluations take 20–30 minutes. We walk the property, assess soil conditions and water patterns, identify the root cause, and give you a clear picture of what's needed. You don't need to prepare anything.
What if my yard isn't a good fit?
We tell you honestly. Some yards have soil percolation rates too poor for any drainage system without additional measures. We'll explain what's causing the issue and point you toward what might actually help. No hard sell, no pressure.
How long does installation take?
Most residential installations are completed in one day. Larger stormwater systems may take two days. We'll give you a specific timeline before any work starts, and we clean up completely when done.
What maintenance does the system require?
None. No moving parts, no filters, no fabric, no gravel to clean or replace. It performs identically on day one and year twenty. Once installed, it's done.
Does it work in Georgia clay soil?
Yes — and it's specifically where pressure-fed drainage outperforms traditional systems most dramatically. Georgia's red clay has very low percolation rates, which is exactly why gravity-based systems fail here. Our pressure-fed design doesn't depend on soil absorption to move water.
